2. Analysis of Impact: Access to Care 
What is the access gap? 
 
The access gap 
Access gap  =  care needed by Medi-Cal members moved from Managed Care to Fee-for-
Service  −  care they can get from providers who accept Medi-Cal Fee-for-Service  
Measure it by comparing demand (Section 2) with supply (Section 3). Expect the largest gaps 
where there are many Medi-Cal members and few Fee-for-Service providers.

2.2 How many of the plan's members will move? 
The number of Inland Empire Health Plan members who will move is not public. Statewide, about 2 
million of more than 14 million Medi-Cal members are moving, about 1 in 7. The Inland Empire share 
may differ from the state average. 
You do not need the exact number. You may make reasonable assumptions and create what-if

3. The supply side: Providers 
3.1 Managed Care Network doctors and Fee-for-Service doctors 
These terms describe how a doctor is paid for Medi-Cal patients. The same doctor can be both: 
• Through the managed care health plan: the doctor contracts with Inland Empire Health Plan 
and the plan pays the doctor. These are the plan's network doctors. 
• Directly by the state: the doctor is enrolled in Medi-Cal Fee-for-Service (FFS) and bills the 
state.

3.2 Other provider data 
• Shortage areas: federally designated areas short of primary care, dental, or mental health 
providers (Source G). 
• Doctor pay: in 2024, Medicaid physician fees nationally were 0.75 of Medicare's, and 0.66 for 
primary care (Medicaid-to-medicare fee index, Ref 6). A national study (2012, Ref 5) found 
doctors were more likely to accept new Medicaid patients in states where Medicaid paid closer 
to Medicare rates. California's value is on the same Medicaid-to-medicare fee index page.

5.2 Notes on each data source 
Source A: Medi-Cal members by ZIP code 
What it is: Monthly counts of people certified eligible for Medi-Cal, by ZIP code and sex, from 2005 
to the most recent month. Counts only, not individual records. Published by the California 
Department of Health Care Services. 
What to look at: 
• Download only "Calendar Years 2020 to the Most Recent Reportable Month." 
• Use August or September 2026 data. 
• Add the counts for both sexes to get total members per ZIP code. 
Notes: 
• Blank cells are values hidden to protect confidentiality (dataset page). Treat them as "not 
shown," not zero. 
• Store ZIP codes as text, not numbers, or matches with other files will fail. 
• If the file has no county column, use the HUD-USPS ZIP code to county crosswalk (Source H) 
to find the ZIP codes in Riverside and San Bernardino counties. 
 
Source B: Medi-Cal members by county, with demographics 
What it is: Monthly county-level counts in separate files by age group and sex, primary language, 
delivery system and plan, and other traits. 
What to look at: 
• By Age Group and Sex and By Primary Language to see which groups are most affected 
and which languages members speak. 
• By Delivery System and Plan for managed care versus fee-for-service counts by county and 
plan.

Notes: 
• Do not use "By Aid Category". It mentions immigration-status (dataset page). 
• County level only: no differences within a county. 
 
Source C: Inland Empire Health Plan fact sheet 
What it is: The plan's published membership counts by program, and # of providers in their network  
What to look at: 
• Membership size, and the share in Medi-Cal; # of providers 
Notes: 
• Dated October 2026; the count month is not stated. 
 
Source D: Medi-Cal managed care enrollment report 
What it is: Monthly managed care enrollment by plan type, county, and health plan. 
What to look at: 
• The plan's enrollment trend by county; comparison with the other plan in the region. 
Notes: 
• May count dual members differently from the fact sheet. 
 
Source E: Medi-Cal fee-for-service providers 
What it is: The state's list of enrolled fee-for-service providers, with provider number, name, type, 
specialty, and location. Excludes managed care providers.  
What to look at: 
• Record the "as of" date. 
Notes: 
• Enrolled does not mean accepting new fee-for-service patients. 
• One provider can appear at several addresses. Check for repeats and do not double count. 
 
Source F: Dental fee-for-service providers and safety net clinics 
What it is: Dentists enrolled in Medi-Cal fee-for-service dental and safety net clinics that provide 
dental services, with ZIP codes. 
What to look at: 
• Repeat the Section 5.1 match for dental care. 
Notes: 
• Updated about twice a year; the current version is as of October 2025 (dataset page).  
 
Source G: Federal shortage areas 
What it is: Areas designated by the Health Resources and Services Administration as short of 
primary care, dental, or mental health providers.

What to look at: 
• Download the primary care, dental, and mental health files; filter to the two counties. 
• Use the shortage score to compare areas: scores range from 1 to 25 for primary care and 
mental health and 1 to 26 for dental, and "the higher the HPSA score, the greater the need" 
(Ref 4). 
Notes: 
• Shortages are measured for all providers, not fee-for-service only. 
• Consider this source as optional, it does not give zip code or city level shortage area 
designations 
• https://www.chcf.org/wp-
content/uploads/2020/07/ShortchangedHealthWorkforceGapsRiversideSanBernardino.pdf (Ref 
8) gives county level numbers on people in shortage areas 
 
 
Source H: ZIP code to county crosswalk 
What it is: Files from the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development that link postal ZIP 
codes to counties and other Census areas, updated quarterly (dataset page). 
What to look at: 
• Download the "ZIP - County" file and keep the rows for Riverside and San Bernardino 
counties. Use this list to filter Sources A and E. 
Notes: 
• The files are free, but the page asks you to register an account first. 
• Some ZIP codes cross county lines. Use your judgement on how to handle.
